# Audio and Voice

## Language rule (hard rule)

The language is determined by the user's prompt:

1. If the prompt specifies a language, use it.
2. If the prompt includes dialogue in a particular language, preserve that
   language for that dialogue.
3. If no language is specified, choose the most appropriate language for the
   prompt and intended audience — and state the choice in the brief.

Never translate supplied dialogue into another language. Never mix languages
within one character's lines unless the prompt does.

## Dialogue

- Preserve supplied dialogue verbatim, including wording and punctuation that
  carries meaning.
- Do not invent unnecessary dialogue. If a scene needs a line the prompt does
  not supply, either leave the scene silent (with sound design carrying it) or
  write the minimum line the story needs — and flag it as skill-written in the
  script.
- Match speech timing to action: a line is spoken while the speaker is on
  screen and facing the camera or the listener, not over an unrelated cut.
- Avoid overlapping dialogue. One speaker at a time unless the prompt stages
  an interruption.
- Emotional delivery matches the scene: shouting is shouted, whispering is
  whispered — in the prompt text, not just in the description.

## Voiceover and narration

- Narration is a separate track in the plan: script, voice character, pace,
  and which visuals it covers.
- Narration must never describe what the picture already shows plainly; it
  adds context, emotion, or information the picture cannot carry.
- Keep narration slower and clearer than conversational speech, with pauses
  at scene boundaries so it never clips mid-sentence at a clip join.

## Voice consistency (honest limit)

Recurring characters keep the same voice identity in prompt text (tone, pace,
accent, speaking style). Muse AI provides no voice-casting or voice-cloning
control, so voice identity between clips can drift. That is a known platform
limit: note it in the QC report when it occurs, do not claim a casting choice
that was not made, and do not promise a specific real voice.

## Music and sound design

- Music fits the scene and the platform: soft emotional beds for drama,
  silence for tension, natural ambience for documentary and realism.
- Dialogue and narration stay louder and clearer than any music bed, always.
- Sound effects are chosen per scene (door, footsteps, water, crowd) and
  timed to the action, never as a generic bed.
- Use generation audio or assets the user supplies. Never claim a licence for
  music you do not hold, and never present a well-known commercial track as
  cleared.

## Silence

Silence is a valid sound design choice. A scene with no dialogue and no music
is not an unfinished scene if the storyboard planned it that way.
