# Long-Form Production

## The core fact

A long video is not a short video stretched. It is a plan that survives many
messages. A 28-minute video is ~168 clips. At 6 clips per user message, that
is 28 messages. The plan must therefore be resumable, honest, and boring in
the best sense: no step depends on remembering something that was not written
down.

## Planning a long video

1. **Structure first.** Divide the story into parts or chapters (not equal
   scenes — story beats). Each part gets its own mini-arc and its own
   storyboard section.
2. **Clip budget.** Total seconds ÷ 10 = clip count. Round up, then adjust
   splits so no clip carries two major story beats.
3. **Batch map.** Assign every clip a batch number (6 per batch). Write the
   batch map into the production plan before generating anything.
4. **Per-part checkpoints.** At each part boundary, verify: all clips present,
   joins inspected, continuity log complete. A part is only "done" when its
   clips are verified — never on the basis of prompts written.
5. **Recap discipline.** At the start of each new message, restate: clips done,
   clips remaining, current part, current continuity state (last frame, last
   position, last line). One paragraph. This is what makes resumption work.

## Hybrid structures

Not every long video is one continuous story. Choose per project:

- **Continuous narrative** — one story, chronological, for drama and storytime.
- **Episodic / chaptered** — self-contained chapters under one theme, for
  education and documentary.
- **Montage-led** — visual sequences carried by narration or music, for
  showcases, travel, and food.
- **Dialogue-led** — scenes built around conversation, for interviews,
  family stories, and comedy.
- **Hybrid** — e.g. a documentary chapter structure with a continuous
  presenter thread. Name the hybrid in the brief; do not drift between
  structures mid-project without a storyboard reason.

## Pacing rules for long-form

- A hook in the first 10 seconds, then a promise of what the video delivers.
- One clear beat per ~60–90 seconds; no beat should run longer than the
  audience can follow without a visual change.
- Recurring visual anchors (the presenter, the location, the signature object)
  return at least once per part so the video feels like one production.
- Endings land: the final part resolves the opening question or story, and
  the last clip is planned as an ending, not as "whatever was left".

## What to tell the user up front (every long project)

- Total clips and total messages required.
- What each message will deliver (a verified batch, not a finished film).
- That partial joins are labelled INCOMPLETE with missing clip numbers.
- That the finished film is delivered only when every clip is verified — or,
  if some clips failed after 3 attempts, as a labelled incomplete version with
  the failure report, never as the complete film.
