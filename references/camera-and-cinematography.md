# Camera and Cinematography

## Principle

Camera serves the prompt, not a house style. Do not make every video cinematic.
A smartphone story shot like a film is wrong; a film story shot like a phone
video is wrong.

## Choosing the approach

| Prompt asks for | Camera approach |
|---|---|
| Cinematic storytelling | Stable, composed shots; wide / medium / close-up grammar; deliberate movement (push-in, pull-back, tracking) |
| Realistic smartphone footage | Natural handheld feel, eye-level framing, simple cuts, no film lighting |
| Documentary | Observational framing, natural light, longer holds, minimal camera movement |
| Animation / stick-figure | Flat, readable framing; clear character staging; simple cuts; no confusing transitions |
| UGC / social short-form | Direct, punchy framing; hook in the first seconds; readable at small size |
| No style stated | Infer from story tone and platform; state the inference in the brief |

## Shot vocabulary (use only what the scene needs)

Wide establishing, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, over-the-shoulder,
low angle, high angle, tracking, follow, point-of-view, static, pan, tilt,
push-in, pull-back, handheld. A shot list is not a checklist — a scene that
needs two static shots does not get a tracking shot because the vocabulary
exists.

## Rules

1. **One camera idea per clip.** A 10-second clip supports one clear camera
   movement, not three.
2. **Camera continuity across joins.** Record camera position and framing in
   the continuity log. The next clip continues from that position unless the
   storyboard plans a cut.
3. **No random jumps.** A camera that teleports between angles without a cut
   reason is a defect.
4. **Readability first.** In animation and short-form, the subject must be
   readable at a glance: no cluttered framing, no subject half out of frame.
5. **Movement must be motivated.** A push-in means rising tension or attention;
   a pull-back means revelation or scale. Unmotivated movement is noise.

## Aspect ratio and framing

Frame for the requested ratio from the first prompt, not as an afterthought.
Vertical 9:16 means vertical staging (stacked subjects, portrait compositions);
horizontal 16:9 means lateral staging. A clip delivered in the wrong ratio is a
failed clip: regenerate it. Never disguise a wrong-ratio clip with blur fill,
black bars, or a crop that cuts the sides and call it native.
